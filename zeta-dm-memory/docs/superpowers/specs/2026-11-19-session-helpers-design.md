# Zeta Memory — the three session helpers

Proposed design, 19 November 2026. Four room exports have been reviewed. Operator approval and verification against a working implementation are pending.

The Scribe writes the session book as play happens. The Checker reads each addition against the story already on record and asks the operator about discrepancies. When a reminder comes due, the Reminder writer tells Zeta the story so far in one paragraph. This is the proposed reading order for the three helpers, including when opening an existing room.

## The Scribe keeps the book

For each player message and storyteller reply, in room order, the Scribe records what happened and the facts it establishes. Each entry points back to the source message and the words that support it. The timeline follows the story's days and times, including flashbacks and reported history. If the story gives no time, the entry leaves it unknown. The ledger records who was where, who came or left, what people knew or promised, what happened to an item and what the party still needs to do.

The Scribe keeps the sequence of changes. Giving away a staff ends one person's custody and begins another's. Lending it may change custody while leaving ownership intact. A character claiming to have guarded it creates an attributed claim. A later account can sit beside an earlier one while the Checker investigates. Discard an attached or echoed reminder block from either speaker's message, then read the remaining story blocks. In The Unchained Princess #892, the storyteller echoes an old reminder before narrating Kira's exit. That exit belongs in the book.

Each room has a last **written** message. It advances only when the entry and its ledger changes have been saved together. A message with no new facts still gets a completed read. If five messages arrive while the Scribe is busy, all five remain owed in order. Reopening the room resumes at the first gap. Existing rooms can be read in historical stretches, retaining individual citations. An old scene summary alone cannot certify that the intervening messages were covered.

## The Checker keeps an independent account

For every addition, the Checker reads the new book entry **and its source passage**. It keeps its own ledger of cited claims, so an omission or mistaken merge by the Scribe can itself be raised. It compares the passage to earlier claims about the people, places, items and events involved, following aliases, former holders and past states across the room's history. Ownership, background, ended facts and events recorded only in the timeline are all eligible. If the relevant history exceeds one model call, read it in parts and retain the supporting passages. The last **checked** message advances only after the comparisons and any findings have been saved. An unreadable answer leaves the check pending.

A finding about conflicting passages shows the specific disagreement, the speakers or narrator, the message numbers and exact words on each side, followed by a question the operator can answer. For an inaccurate book entry, show the entry alongside the source. For an omission, quote the source and name the missing fact. A disputed account remains attributed. The operator can select an account, explain how both fit, or leave it open. The Scribe applies a ruling to the book and the Checker records which discrepancy it settles. Re-reading the same evidence leaves one finding. A later incompatible passage can reopen it with the new evidence.

An edited or regenerated source invalidates the affected entries, checks and prepared reminder. Catch-up resumes from the earliest affected message against the current version. If the evidence behind a ruling changed, preserve the ruling's history and ask whether it still applies. Until confirmed, the book and reminder treat the point as open.

For each claim the Checker verifies the subject, action, source words, and story time. A sentence that combines three claims may need three citations. This also applies to changes made from operator rulings. A correct ruling attached to the wrong person is a book error. An open question, a character's private belief, and a witnessed event retain their distinct meanings in the ledger.

### Sera's two forms — accept both measurements

The operator's rulings screenshot shows Sera's build as “five foot nine in human form” (#148) and “twenty meters long in full dragon form” (#196). **Accept both at #196.** The Scribe records human standing height and full-dragon body length as separate, cited facts about Sera. Both remain established across transformations. The book creates no competing-value ruling, the Checker raises no finding, and a reminder that needs her size uses the measurement for the form shown in that scene.

Compare measurements when they describe the same form, dimension and story time, accounting for equivalent units and stated changes. For example, a later claim that Sera was twelve metres long in that same full-dragon scene would warrant checking against #196. Her human height supplies no evidence for that dispute.

Numbers refer to messages in the named room. **Raise** means show the exact conflict or book error. **Ask** means the passages leave more than one credible explanation and the distinction matters. **Accept** means write the event, correction, or ruling and proceed. Judge at the indicated point in the story. Later evidence can resolve an earlier question.

## The Reminder writer owns a due reminder until it is ready

At the configured interval or on Force reminder, record that a paragraph is due for the latest storyteller reply. A due reminder immediately starts any missing reads or joins work already in progress. The writer uses the current book, the checked ledger and rulings to explain how the party got here, what happened, who is where, and what comes next. Include material uncertainties as uncertainties. The operator can inspect or edit the paragraph before it goes through the existing send path.

| At the table | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| A reminder is due at #896. The previous live scene was #894. | Queue the due paragraph and check each helper's saved progress. In the actual export the book covers #1–863. Read #864–896 in order and let the Checker catch up from its own first gap. In the simpler case where the book really covers #1–894, read #895 and #896. The panel shows which read is underway. |
| #895 saves. The read of #896 fails or its answer is unusable. | Written progress stays at #895. Retry recoverable failures with a bounded backoff. If retries are exhausted, or a key or budget blocks reading, show the reason and retain the pending job. Retry or removal of the blocker resumes it. |
| The Scribe saves #896. | The Checker finishes every outstanding comparison through #896. A saved open question counts as a completed check. A failed check remains owed. |
| Both helpers have covered #896. | Write one paragraph using only evidence available through #896. Validate the actual text that will be attached, after shortening or formatting. It must convey current location, attendance, material developments, plans and relevant rulings. A refusal, offer to summarize, stale paragraph or unsupported assertion leaves the reminder pending. Retry a neutral plot summary from the verified facts without repeating successful reads. Persistent failure follows the bounded retry and visible-blocker rules above. |
| A newer reply, a source edit or a relevant ruling arrives before use. | Keep the same due reminder and refresh it against the latest checked book and rulings. Validate that version again before attachment. Preserve any operator-edited wording as a visible draft alongside the refreshed paragraph, so the operator can review and reapply edits. Only a current paragraph is eligible to attach. Repeated ticks, Force presses and reloads must not attach it twice. |
